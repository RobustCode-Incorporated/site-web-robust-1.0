# PRODUCT_SPEC — ROBUST PROJECT OS

Following `docs/digital-products/PRODUCT_DEVELOPMENT_FRAMEWORK.md`.

## 01 — Problem
Most SME founders and independent consultants run projects out of scattered documents, group chats, and memory. Nothing looks wrong until scope quietly expands, a stakeholder is surprised by a decision, or a deliverable ships without anyone having agreed what "done" meant. The recurring cost: rework, strained client relationships, and projects that succeed despite the process rather than because of it.

## 02 — Audience
- Founders running their first structured project without a PMO.
- Independent consultants who need a professional-looking, repeatable process for client work.
- Internal project leads at SMEs without a dedicated project management function.

## 03 — Desired Outcome
The buyer can take a project from a one-paragraph idea to a documented, agreed scope, a realistic plan, and a closure process, using the same structure every time — so project quality doesn't depend on who happens to be running it.

## 04 — Method
Most project failure traces back to a small number of skipped steps (undocumented scope, unmanaged risk, no shared definition of done), not to a lack of effort. A fixed 13-stage checklist-and-template structure forces those steps to happen in order, every time, without requiring the buyer to be a certified project manager.

## 05 — System Design (the 13 stages)
01 Discovery · 02 Objectives · 03 Stakeholders · 04 Scope · 05 Requirements · 06 Deliverables · 07 Dependencies · 08 Risks · 09 Planning · 10 Execution · 11 Monitoring · 12 Validation · 13 Closure.

## 06 — Components (target — see README.md for current progress)
Per stage: a short rationale (why it matters), a template or checklist, and — for stages where it genuinely helps — a structured AI prompt to draft a first version for human review.

Buyer self-check: at the end of each stage, the corresponding checklist item set should be fully checkable without needing to guess. If a buyer can't complete a checklist item, that's a signal the previous stage was rushed, not that the checklist is wrong.
